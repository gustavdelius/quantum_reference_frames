Measurement and reference frames in quantum field theory

Interactive companions to two papers. The first explains how a local measurement of a quantum field works. The second builds on it: measured relative to a quantum reference frame, the algebra of local observables changes type. Each paper has two guides that cover the same material in different ways.

The papers, in reading order
Paper 01 · 2020

C. J. Fewster and R. Verch · Commun. Math. Phys. 378, 851–889 (2020) · doi:10.1007/s00220-020-03800-6

Quantum Fields and Local Measurements

How to measure a quantum field: couple it to a second field, the probe, inside a bounded region, and read the probe later. The paper derives the system observable this measures, where it is localised, how the state updates without any collapse surface, and why successive measurements fit together consistently.

Paper 02 · 2025

C. J. Fewster, D. W. Janssen, L. D. Loveridge, K. Rejzner and J. Waldron · Commun. Math. Phys. 406:19 (2025) · doi:10.1007/s00220-024-05180-7

Quantum Reference Frames, Measurement Schemes and the Type of Local Algebras in QFT

Builds on paper 01. On a background with symmetries you need a reference frame to say which experiment you did. Making that frame quantum and keeping only invariant observables yields a crossed product, and for a thermal frame the type III1 local algebra becomes type II1. This gives an operational account of the de Sitter result of Chandrasekaran, Longo, Penington and Witten.
